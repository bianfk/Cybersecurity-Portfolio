# 📘 Chapter 16: Security Governance and Compliance

📅 **Date Completed:** 2026-04-13  
📚 **Topic:** Policies, standards, procedures, change management, compliance, and vendor risk

---

## 🔑 Key Concept

- **Governance:** The framework of policies, standards, procedures, and guidelines that direct security activities. Boards, committees, and management provide oversight.

- **Policies vs. Standards vs. Procedures:** Policies are high-level (what), standards are specific (how well), procedures are step-by-step (how). Each serves a different role.

- **Change Management:** Formal process for approving and implementing changes — approval, impact analysis, testing, backout plan, SOP, and version control. Prevents unauthorized changes that create vulnerabilities.

- **Compliance:** Adhering to laws, regulations, and industry standards (GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS). Non-compliance leads to fines, sanctions, reputational damage, and loss of license.

- **Third-Party Risk:** Vendors, suppliers, MSPs introduce risk. Assessments, SLAs, MOUs, NDAs, and monitoring are essential. Right-to-audit clauses protect the organization.

---

## 🛠️ Tools/Techniques

- Governance structure (Boards, committees, centralized/decentralized)
- Policies (AUP, Information security, BC/DR, IR, SDLC, Change management)
- Standards (Password, Access control, Physical security, Encryption)
- Procedures (Change management, Onboarding/offboarding, Playbooks)
- Change management (Approval, impact analysis, backout plan, SOP, version control)
- Third-party risk (Vendor assessment, SLA, MOU, MSA, NDA, BPA)
- Compliance (Internal/external, Due diligence, Attestation, Automation)
- Security awareness training (Phishing campaigns, user guidance, reporting)

---

## 🛡️ SOC Relevance

- Policies drive SOC operations — playbooks, escalation paths, and reporting requirements.
- Change management logs correlate with incidents (unapproved changes = red flag).
- Compliance incidents (data breach, non-compliance) have legal and financial impacts.
- Vendor relationships introduce supply chain risk — monitor third-party access and activity.
- User awareness training reduces phishing success — track campaign metrics.

---

## 📝 Summary

Security doesn't exist in a vacuum — governance is the rulebook. I learned that policies enable security, and compliance is the stick that enforces it. For a SOC Analyst, understanding policies means knowing when to escalate, when to contain, and how to report.
